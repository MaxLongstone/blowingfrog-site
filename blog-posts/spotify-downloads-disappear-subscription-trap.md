# Tim Curry Died and Spotify Wouldn't Let Me Play Him

**Date:** September 17, 2026
**Category:** Musings / Money / Culture
**Read time:** ~12 min
**File:** blog/spotify-downloads-disappear-subscription-trap.html

---

Tim Curry died August 26th. Went for a run three weeks later to play Rocky Horror. Downloads were gone — the library listed them, none would play.

Reason: Spotify's 30-day offline rule. "You need to go online at least once every 30 days to keep your downloads." Stated rationale: licensing/royalty data collection. The word "download" implies ownership; what you actually have is a revocable 30-day lease with no warning before expiry.

## Key arguments

- **Owning → access → permission**: Each step removes a right. Permission can be withdrawn without notice.
- **The number gap**: Americans guess $86/month in subscriptions; actual average is $219 (C+R Research). 89% underestimate (West Monroe). 60% have at least one unused sub; 70% forgot to cancel a free trial.
- **LA Fitness FTC suit (Aug 20, 2025)**: In-person or certified mail to cancel. One authorized employee per club. Cancellation hours during work hours. Rebilled under new account numbers after stop-payment.
- **Click-to-cancel rule vacated**: Eighth Circuit killed FTC Negative Option Rule July 8, 2025, on procedural grounds. FTC restarted process Jan 30, 2026. No rule in force.
- **John Oliver**: LWT S12E22, Aug 23, 2025. "Universally annoying problem." A year ago. Nothing changed.
- **Self-rebuttal**: Access genuinely beats ownership on math. CD shelf was a liability. 30-day rule is licensing, not malice. Argument narrows to: the word "download" was doing work it hadn't earned, and there was no reason not to warn on day 29.
