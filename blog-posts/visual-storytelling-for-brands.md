The brands people remember don't just show products — they show a world. Here's how to build a visual language that makes every frame feel like yours.

## What a Point of View Actually Means

A brand point of view isn't a style guide. It's a consistent way of seeing the world that shows up in everything — the angle of a shot, the color of a shadow, the type of person you cast, whether you show the mess or the clean version of life. Apple shows the clean version. Patagonia shows the mess. Both are intentional.

## Story Before Product

The biggest mistake brands make: leading with the product. Nobody wakes up thinking about your product. They wake up thinking about their life, their problems, their aspirations. The brands that earn attention start with that world first and let the product enter it naturally.

## Consistency Is the Superpower

Nike's visual language is immediately recognizable across a 30-second spot, a poster in a Tokyo subway, and a post on a 22-year-old's Instagram. That recognition didn't happen by accident. It happened because they made consistent decisions over decades about light, motion, casting, music, and pacing.

Consistency is not boring. Inconsistency is invisible.

## Building Your Visual World

Start with three questions:
1. What does your brand believe that most brands don't?
2. What kind of life does your product belong in?
3. What does your customer aspire to feel — not just have?

The answers to these questions are your visual brief. Everything else — color palette, typography, photography style — is execution.
