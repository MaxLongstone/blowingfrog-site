## Key arguments

- **Casting window vs. mirror**: Franchise horror casts windows (Vera Farmiga/Patrick Wilson). Outrage advertising buys mirrors. Same industry, opposite optimizations.
- **The receipt**: AEO stock +25% after the jeans ad. Then June 2026 earnings: brand sales down 2%, driven by women's bottoms. The category she was selling.
- **Attention and conversion are now separately priced**: Outrage is cheap for attention, structurally bad for conversion — same mechanism that generates the argument makes buyers not want to be seen in the product.
- **Right play, wrong company**: Novig (name recognition stage) ran the correct play. AEO (unit-sales stage) ran the wrong one. Same strategy, different companies, one was right.
- **Vice companies**: You cannot ask a gambling company to advertise responsibly. That's asking it to be worse at its only job. Tobacco analogy holds.
- **How to stop it**: Regulate (slow, works eventually) or make the ad boring (nobody will).
- **Oscar Wilde, 1890**: "There is only one thing in the world worse than being talked about, and that is not being talked about." — Dorian Gray. Not Oliver (my phone's error — kept as a gag).

## LinkedIn version

This conference room ran an hour over a jeans commercial. I want to tell you why that mattered.

I was in a meeting with a big tech client — chronographed, fifteen minutes, walk you out at fourteen and change. Seven minutes in, the most serious person at the table asked if I'd seen the American Eagle Sydney Sweeney ad. The tech stack was never mentioned again.

Two weeks later, different room, I learned that the film algorithm we were reviewing was explicitly optimizing against what that ad had bought. Horror franchises pay a premium to make sure nobody in a couple feels threatened by the face on screen. American Eagle paid a premium to make sure everyone did. Same summer. Opposite bets.

Here's the reconciliation: attention and conversion are now separately priced products. The AEO campaign bought a 25% stock surge. It did not buy jeans. By June 2026, brand sales were down 2%, driven by women's bottoms — the exact category she was selling.

So Novig, launching last week with a nude Sydney Sweeney ad and getting exactly the backlash it wanted, is running the right play — for a startup that needs name recognition. American Eagle ran the same play for a retailer that needed units. One of them was right.

Oscar Wilde wrote the operating principle in 1890. It's still billing.
