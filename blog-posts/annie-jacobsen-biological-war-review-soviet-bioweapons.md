# Biological War by Annie Jacobsen: The Book That Broke Me

**Date:** September 16, 2026
**Category:** Books / Musings
**Read time:** ~13 min
**File:** blog/annie-jacobsen-biological-war-review-soviet-bioweapons.html

---

I read horror the way a large man eats pastry. King, Barker, Stephen Graham Jones, Dan Simmons, The Terror. I'm calibrated. Hard to scare. Then Annie Jacobsen published Nuclear War: A Scenario two years ago and installed something that still wakes me up at 3am thinking about North Korea.

Then this year she did it worse.

Biological War: A Scenario. Dutton. July 2026. 432 pages.

## Why the slow one is worse

Nuclear War: horror of velocity. 26 minutes, no decision window. Ends in an hour because the world does.

Biological War: same annihilation, stretched. Engineered pathogen. Pneumonic plague — no flea, no rat, just a person breathing near another person. Hour by hour, day by day, week by week. Public health can't identify it. Government can't attribute it. Quarantine can't hold. Vaccines don't exist yet.

Doesn't end with a flash. Ends with a depopulated, militarized country, civil institutions rotted out.

Tom Daschle — Senate Majority Leader who had anthrax mailed to his own office in 2001 — says it: "a long, painful, slow death. Death physically. Death governmentally." The institution dies too. Slowly. In public.

## These are not novels

Nonfiction. Jacobsen's definition: "a fictional sequence of events constructed using interviews, documentary research and established government procedures and technologies." Only the trigger is invented. The machine is real. The only made-up part is which Tuesday.

## The room was absolutely silent

1989. Soviet lab. Scientists demonstrate a weapon producing "symptoms of two different diseases, one of which could not be traced." Jacobsen's line: "The room was absolutely silent." No adjectives. Just the temperature of a room where professionals have understood what they made.

## The program that shadows every page

Biopreparat: founded 1973, nominally civilian, 52 sites, 50,000+ employees. Signed the Biological Weapons Convention in 1972. Built the largest bioweapons program in history the following year.

Ken Alibek, first deputy director 1988, defected 1992: tularemia bomb, Strain 836 ("most virulent anthrax known to man"), hundreds of tons of smallpox, deliverable by ballistic missile.

Eleven agents weaponized. Anthrax. Plague. Tularemia. Glanders. Brucellosis. Q-fever. Venezuelan equine encephalitis. Botulinum. Staph B. Smallpox. Marburg.

Projects: Ecology (agricultural weapons), Bonfire (antibiotic resistance), Factor (high-virulence, novel presentation). India-1967 smallpox strain sourced from the WHO eradication campaign.

## Three things that actually happened

- Vozrozhdeniya Island, 1971: open-air smallpox test, carrier reached Aralsk. Containment strategy: cancel the train stop.
- Sverdlovsk, April 1979: weaponized anthrax escaped military facility, 105+ dead, KGB destroyed hospital records.
- Nikolai Ustinov: infected working on Marburg, died, strain from his organs more potent than working strain. Named Variant U. Approved for weaponization 1990.
